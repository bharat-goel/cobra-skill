What was built

Two skills, deliberately narrow

A “systems thinking” skill described as use when thinking about problems would load on every task, consume context, and degrade unrelated work. Each of these carries a concrete trigger situation instead, and the first carries an explicit negative trigger.

system-triage

Fires when
Starting an ambiguous or multi-step problem — an unfamiliar bug, an architecture decision, a migration, an incident. Or when work is looping and repeated fixes aren’t holding.
Stays silent
On tasks that already have an obvious established path.
Does
Classifies the problem into one of four domains via DART, then applies the protocol that matches.

incentive-check

Fires when
Defining or changing a test, eval, benchmark, coverage target, KPI, alert threshold, lint rule, or agent success condition.
Also when
A metric is improving but the goal is not — a green suite over wrong behavior.
Does
Names the cheapest way the measure can be satisfied without achieving the goal, before it gets adopted.

The framework

Four domains, split by one line

The arrangement below is Snowden’s own: unordered domains on the left, ordered on the right, disorder at the centre. Crossing that line wrongly is the expensive mistake — demanding a root cause before acting on emergent behaviour, or guessing at something you could simply have read the source for.

Unordered — cause and effect only after acting Ordered — knowable before acting

Unordered

Complex

Cause and effect visible only in hindsight.

  • Smallest reversible probe first. Failing test, feature flag, one component, a single migrated record.
  • Probe → observe → amplify what worked, damp what didn’t.
  • Directionally right beats precisely right.

Ordered

Complicated

Hidden, but knowable if you dig.

  • Don’t guess. Pattern-matching a plausible fix before understanding the mechanism is the failure mode.
  • Read the real source, trace the real call path, inspect the actual data.
  • Get the right specialist, not any specialist.

Unordered

Chaotic

The link is broken. Cannot be known now.

  • Stabilise first. Revert, restore green, stop the write, halt the migration.
  • For an agent, “act first” means the safe reversible action — never a drastic unverified one.
  • Once stable, re-triage. Don’t stay in crisis mode after the crisis.

Ordered

Clear

Obvious. An established pattern exists.

  • Match the existing pattern exactly. Inventing a novel approach here is itself the defect.
  • Canary: if small conventions slipped, the larger contract wasn’t read either.
  • Watch the cliff — complacency here is what precedes the outage.

Centre — the default state

Disorder. Not knowing which domain you are in. Snowden calls it the most common condition, and it is invisible from the inside: everyone simply applies their favourite approach. The analyst analyses, the firefighter fights fires, the process person writes a checklist. Exit by deconstructing until one sub-part can answer the cause-and-effect question. Different sub-parts routinely sit in different domains.

The video omits all three of these. Disorder, the ordered/unordered split, and the clear→chaotic cliff are in Snowden’s original and were restored here — they are the parts that matter most for agentic work.

The diagnostic

DART

Cynefin describes the domains but says less about how you work out which one you are currently in. DART is Sandeep Swadia’s contribution, and it fills exactly that gap.

  • DDeconstruct

    What are the parts? Stable, or shifting while you look at them? Shifting parts push you toward the unordered side.

  • AAnalyze

    What is the relationship between cause and effect? This single question assigns the domain.

  • RRecognize

    Have I seen this pattern before — here, or in another system? Often faster than fresh analysis.

  • TTest

    Run the smallest test that could disconfirm the classification. Except in chaos, where there is no time.

Fidelity

What this adaptation changed

Recorded so the differences from both sources stay visible. Two are corrections; the rest are restorations and translations.

  • Softened “you cannot hire an expert” for complex systems The video states experts don’t help in complex systems. Overstated — experts sharpen probes and read results faster; what they can’t supply is a guaranteed prescription. Encoding the stronger claim would make an agent discard useful input.
  • Constrained “act first” in chaotic systems Sound advice for a human in a crisis, hazardous as an instruction to an agent. Scoped here to safe, reversible, stabilising actions — revert, restore, halt — never a drastic unverified change.
  • Restored disorder, the ordered/unordered split, and the clear→chaotic cliff All three are in Snowden’s original and absent from the video. They carry most of the framework’s value for agentic work.
  • Translated all four protocols to software Match the existing pattern / trace the real source / smallest reversible probe / restore green first. This mapping is the adaptation’s own, present in neither source.
  • Connected the cobra effect to reward hacking An agent told “make the tests pass” has been handed a measure, and the cheapest path to satisfying it is frequently not the path that does the work. Same structure, named in the AI safety literature as specification gaming.
  • Dropped the motivational and biographical material Doesn’t belong in a skill. Preserved in the captured transcript instead.

Sourcing note. The video presents the four domains as “the four types of systems” without naming Cynefin or Snowden. The correspondence is exact — the same four names, including the post-2015 “clear”, and the same four prescriptions — so the domains are credited to Snowden throughout. DART does appear to be Swadia’s own. This is recorded as a sourcing observation, not an accusation.

Historicity caveat. The Delhi cobra-bounty story is described in the literature as an anecdote, not a documented historical event. Siebert used it to name the phenomenon. Worth repeating as illustration; worth not asserting as history. The Van Halen and Tylenol cases, by contrast, are well documented.

Where it lives

Installed and running

The repository sits at ~/code/agent-systems-thinking on branch feat/initial-skills, symlinked into ~/.claude/skills/ so edits take effect immediately.

ln -sfn "$PWD/skills/system-triage"   ~/.claude/skills/system-triage
ln -sfn "$PWD/skills/incentive-check" ~/.claude/skills/incentive-check

To share it later, add .claude-plugin/marketplace.json at the root and a plugins/ wrapper; others then install with /plugin marketplace add. Nothing in the skills themselves needs to change.
