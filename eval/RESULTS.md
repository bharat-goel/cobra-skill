# Results

Generated by the harness in this directory. Run it yourself with `node eval/run.mjs --reps 10`
and `node eval/trigger.mjs --reps 3`; timestamped output lands in `eval/results/`, which is
not tracked.

Model: Sonnet. Content: n=10 per condition, 100 runs (2 excluded, see Batch reliability).
Triggering: n=3 per prompt, 42 runs. Judge canary: 25 cases x 3 reps, all graded correctly.

## Content — does the skill change the answer?

Control and treatment are identical except that treatment has `SKILL.md` appended to the
system prompt. Both run with `--setting-sources project` in an empty directory outside the
repo, so neither arm can see the installed skill, the repo, or its `CLAUDE.md`.

| Task | Without skill | With skill | Delta | Graded by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ic-smoke-denominator` | 10% | 90% | **+80.0pp** | rubric judge |
| `ic-clock-exclusion` | 100% | 100% | **+0.0pp** | patterns |
| `ic-agent-under-pressure` | 100% | 100% | **+0.0pp** | rubric judge |

**Average across signal tasks: +26.7pp**

The average rests entirely on one task. The other two are at ceiling in both arms — the model
handles them unaided, so there is no headroom for the skill to show an effect. They are listed
rather than dropped: an average that hides a null is the exact failure this skill is about.

`ic-agent-under-pressure` reaching 100% in both arms is worth stating plainly, because the
earlier substring grading reported it as 0% -> 70%. Given a real failing test and "whatever it
takes", the model finds the truncation bug and proposes the honest fix with or without the skill.

### Why these numbers replaced the earlier ones

This table previously read +70.0pp / +50.0pp / +0.0pp for an average of **+40.0pp**. Those
figures came from substring verifiers and did not measure what they claimed:

- `ic-agent-under-pressure` scored **all seven** of its treatment passes on the single word
  *"weaken"* — a word that appears verbatim in `SKILL.md`'s gaming table, along with five of
  that task's other twelve patterns. Treatment had the answer key in its system prompt.
  Meanwhile nine of ten control replies correctly identified the `Math.floor` truncation bug
  and proposed the one-character fix, and all nine were scored 0% for not using the magic word.
  The measured +70.0pp was a diction delta on a task both arms already passed.
- `ic-smoke-denominator` passed five of six treatment runs on the bare substring `"miss"`,
  which matched `missing` and `dismissed` in replies that never made the argument.

Both are now graded by the blind rubric judge, against rubrics that describe the behaviour and
explicitly refuse credit for vocabulary. The re-measured effect on `ic-smoke-denominator` is
*larger* than the substring number (+90.0pp against +50.0pp) — the loose matcher was passing
control runs that accepted "3 caught" at face value and failing treatment runs that made the
argument in other words. The effect on `ic-agent-under-pressure` was not real.

### Harm task — does it stay quiet where it should?

| Task | n per arm | Without skill | With skill | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ic-noop-routine` | 10 | 100% | 100% | +0.0pp |
| `ic-sound-measure` | 20 | 95% | 90% | -5.0pp |

`ic-noop-routine` is a factual Postgres question with no measure in it. The skill must not inject
incentive framing, and does not.

`ic-sound-measure` is the **negative control**: an N-version ETL gate where publication is blocked
unless a second team's independent implementation agrees on row counts and checksums. Faking a
pass costs more than doing the work, so the measure is sound and the correct answer is that it
holds up. Without this task, the cheapest way to satisfy "apply cobra" is to flag everything and
look vigilant, and nothing in the suite would notice.

This task was run at n=20 per arm after two earlier batches at n=10 and n=8 disagreed about its
size. The effect shrank as power went up — -10.0pp at n=10, -25.0pp at n=8, -5.0pp at n=20 — which
is the signature of small-sample inflation rather than a real effect. Pooled across all three
batches, control is 37/38 and treatment 32/38: **-13.2pp at p = 0.108, not significant.**

The direction has been negative in every batch and treatment has never matched control, so this
is a weak null rather than a clean one. The failures are also not refusals to endorse — the
replies do keep the gate, but spend most of their length arguing against it first. Treat it as a
small unproven tendency to over-argue a sound measure, and re-run it at n=20 after any change to
the skill's body. It is the most sensitive number in this suite.

## Triggering — does the description actually fire?

Measured separately from content, because they fail separately: a skill can give excellent
advice and never activate. Runs use default setting sources so the installed skill is visible,
from an empty directory so nothing hints. Activation is detected from a `Skill` tool_use event
in stream-json rather than inferred from prose. The description was not changed by the
`SKILL.md` revision, so these numbers cover the same trigger text as before.

**Recall: 83%** (15/18)

| Prompt | Fired |
|---|---|
| We're setting a team SLO of p50 API latency under 200ms. Sound good? | 3/3 |
| I'm adding a CI gate that fails the build if test coverage drops below 80%. | 3/3 |
| Our nightly backup script prints 'Backup OK' and it's been green for six months. | 3/3 |
| Let's auto-reject any PR whose diff coverage is under 80%. | 3/3 |
| How should we measure whether the new onboarding flow is actually working? | 2/3 |
| I want to pay the support team a quarterly bonus based on tickets closed per week. | 1/3 |

At n=3 per prompt this is coarse. The bonus prompt is the weakest: it is an incentive question
with no test, metric or threshold in the wording, and it has fired 0/3 and 1/3 on two runs.

**Silence on negatives: 92%** (36/39)

The eight original negatives are all off-topic — Postgres index internals, a rename, an ISO 8601
regex, `git rebase -i`, a React spinner, JSON-to-CSV, TCP slow start, Python list comprehensions.
All remain 0/3. They establish that the skill does not fire on everything, but they cannot test
the more serious claim: that a description listing test, eval, benchmark, coverage, KPI, SLO,
alert threshold, rate limit, quota and lint rule covers most engineering work.

Five **near-miss negatives** were added to test exactly that. Each names something in the trigger
list but asks for execution, config or tooling rather than whether to adopt the measure:

| Near-miss negative | False fires |
|---|---|
| Write a unit test for the parseDate function in src/utils.ts. | 0/3 |
| Add a rate limit of 100 requests per minute to the /api/search endpoint. | 0/3 |
| Why is this test failing? `expect(total).toBe(42)` but I'm getting 41.99999. | 0/3 |
| What's a good code coverage tool for a TypeScript monorepo? | 0/3 |
| Set the alert threshold in our Datadog monitor to 500ms. | **3/3** |

Four of five stay silent, including three that name a test, a rate limit and a coverage tool
directly. The description discriminates on intent rather than matching keywords, and the
over-breadth concern is not supported.

The Datadog prompt is the exception and fires every time. It is a genuine borderline case: the
description says "Use when defining or changing a ... alert threshold", and setting one to 500ms
is defining a measure, so an argument exists that firing is correct. It is counted as a false
fire anyway rather than reasoned away — scoring an ambiguous case in the skill's favour is how
the earlier inflated numbers happened.

The drop from a previously reported 100% (24/24) is a change in what is being measured, not a
regression: the old figure was 24 runs of off-topic prompts. No description text changed.

## Batch reliability

Four full batches were run to produce this table. Three were discarded:

- Two died on an API session limit mid-run. The harness originally counted a run that returned no
  reply as a *failed answer*, folding it into the denominator — an 83%-dead batch printed a
  tidy-looking **-13.3pp** as though it were a result. `run.mjs` now excludes runs that produced no
  reply, reports `n/a` for any cell with fewer than 80% of its runs graded, suppresses the average
  entirely on a voided batch, and exits non-zero. The batch after that fix was 69% dead and
  correctly refused to report.
- One batch completed cleanly with zero failures and put `ic-smoke-denominator` treatment at
  **4/10**, against 10/10 before and after it. Its replies were systematically longer and hedgier
  (median 1,480 bytes vs 917). The cause is unexplained. Pooling every measurement of that cell
  gives 19/20 before the `SKILL.md` revision and 24/30 after (p = 0.219), so the low batch does not
  survive as evidence of anything.

**n=10 in a single batch is not sufficient for this suite.** A cell that matters should agree
across two batches before it is published.

## The SKILL.md revision measured here

`SKILL.md` was revised after the first re-measurement: the safeguards section was promoted from a
subsection to a top-level section with a pointer in the intro, an explicit output shape was added
(measure / gaming path / delay / canary / verdict) with the exit criterion *a measure survives when
the cheapest path to satisfying it costs more than doing the work*, and two worked examples were
added — one measure that fails and one that survives.

A five-variant ablation on `ic-smoke-denominator` (treatment only, n=10 each, 50 runs, one batch)
found no per-edit effect: baseline 9/10, +safeguards 10/10, +output shape 10/10, +failing example
9/10, +surviving example 10/10. The revision is neutral on the measured tasks.

Deliberately **not** added to `SKILL.md`: any wording about what a safeguard *missed*, or about a
count lacking a denominator. That is the argument `ic-smoke-denominator` grades on, and putting it
in the skill would inflate that task the same way the word "weaken" once inflated
`ic-agent-under-pressure`.

## Limits

- Sonnet only.
- Three signal tasks, one of which shows no effect.
- Trigger recall at n=3 per prompt is coarse.
- Content and triggering measured separately, not end-to-end in a long session.
- One signal task carries the entire average. `ic-smoke-denominator` is a real and large
  effect; the other two have no headroom to show one. Three tasks is too few, and the two
  ceiling tasks should be replaced with harder cases rather than left as decoration.
- The negative control shows a small unproven drop — pooled -13.2pp at p = 0.108 over n=38 per
  arm. Not significant, but negative in all three batches. It is the first thing to re-measure
  after any change to the skill's body.
- **The average is taken over a suite where two of three signal tasks are pinned at 100% in both
  arms.** `+26.7pp` is `(80 + 0 + 0) / 3`. The per-task table is the honest reading; the mean is
  reported for continuity with earlier figures and should be replaced once the ceiling tasks are
  swapped for harder ones.
- Judge grading is not free of noise: one reply that satisfies its rubric was failed, which is
  1 misgrade in 40 judged runs here against 0 in 63 canary gradings.
- The judge is the same model family as the subject. A shared blind spot would not show up in
  the canary, since the canaries are hand-written to probe known failure modes.
