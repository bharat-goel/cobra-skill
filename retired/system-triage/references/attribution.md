# Attribution — system-triage

Nothing in this skill is an original idea. This file records where each part came from.

## The four domains: Cynefin — Dave Snowden

The clear / complicated / complex / chaotic model is the **Cynefin framework**, created in
**1999 by Dave Snowden** while at IBM Global Services.

Canonical sources:

- Kurtz, C. F., & Snowden, D. J. (2003). "The new dynamics of strategy: Sense-making in a
  complex and complicated world." *IBM Systems Journal*, 42(3), 462–483.
- Snowden, D. J., & Boone, M. E. (November 2007). "A Leader's Framework for Decision Making."
  *Harvard Business Review*.
- Snowden, D., Goh, Z., & Borchardt, S. (2020). *Cynefin — Weaving Sense-Making into the
  Fabric of Our World.* The Cynefin Company.
- Overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynefin_framework

Terminology note: the first domain was originally **simple**, renamed **obvious** in 2014 and
**clear** in 2015. Kurtz & Snowden (2003) originally named the domains *known, knowable,
complex, chaotic*.

Snowden's response patterns, used here in adapted form:

| Domain | Snowden's sequence |
|---|---|
| Clear | sense – categorize – respond |
| Complicated | sense – analyze – respond |
| Complex | probe – sense – respond |
| Chaotic | act – sense – respond |

## The DART diagnostic: Sandeep Swadia

**D**econstruct / **A**nalyze / **R**ecognize / **T**est is from
**Sandeep Swadia**, "How To Think SO Clearly People Assume You're Brilliant"
(YouTube, 2026) — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mjTgkm-h__M

This appears to be Swadia's own contribution. Cynefin describes the domains but is thinner
on the question of how you determine which one you are currently in. DART fills that gap.

Note: the video presents the four domains as "the four types of systems" without naming
Cynefin or Snowden. The correspondence is exact — same four names including the post-2015
"clear", and the same four prescriptions — so the domains are credited to Snowden above.
This is recorded as a sourcing observation, not an accusation.

## The illustrations

- **Van Halen's brown M&M's** — recounted by **David Lee Roth** in his autobiography
  *Crazy From the Heat* (1997). Roth explained the clause himself as a contract-compliance
  canary. Well documented.
- **Chicago Tylenol murders (1982)** and Johnson & Johnson's recall of ~31 million bottles —
  historical event, widely documented.
- **The firefighter reading smoke**, the coffee-shop system, and the train-platform
  perspective illusion — illustrations from Swadia's video. The train illusion itself is a
  known perceptual phenomenon (induced motion / vection).

## What this adaptation changed, and why

Recorded so the differences from both sources are visible:

1. **Restored the fifth state, "disorder."** Snowden includes disorder/confusion — not knowing
   which domain you are in — and calls it the default condition. The video omits it. It is the
   most useful part of the framework for agentic work, so it is restored here.

2. **Restored the ordered / unordered distinction.** Snowden groups clear + complicated as
   *ordered* and complex + chaotic as *unordered*. The video omits this. It is the deep
   structure of the model and names the most expensive category of error.

3. **Named the clear→chaotic cliff.** Snowden warns that complacency in the clear domain
   causes sudden collapse into chaos. The video does not cover it.

4. **Softened "you cannot hire an expert" for complex systems.** The video states that experts
   do not help in complex systems. This is overstated: experts sharpen probes and read results
   faster; what they cannot supply is a guaranteed prescription. Encoding the stronger claim
   would make an agent discard useful input.

5. **Constrained "act first" in chaotic systems.** The video's advice — act before analyzing —
   is sound for humans in a crisis but hazardous as an instruction to an agent. Here it is
   scoped to *safe, reversible, stabilizing* actions: revert, restore, halt. Never a drastic
   unverified change.

6. **Translated all four protocols to software and agentic work.** The domain protocols in the
   skill (match the existing pattern / trace the real source / smallest reversible probe /
   restore green first) are this adaptation's mapping, not present in either source.

7. **Dropped the motivational and biographical material** — it does not belong in a skill.
   It is preserved in the companion write-up instead.

8. **Promoted sub-part triage into the Deconstruct step.** Decomposing a problem and finding its
   parts in different domains is standard Cynefin practice, not new. What is this adaptation's
   own is the *placement and emphasis*: it was originally a closing line in the disorder section
   and was moved into DART's first step after a usage test showed it was the move that actually
   resolved a stalled decision. Recorded because the change was driven by evidence rather than
   by either source.
